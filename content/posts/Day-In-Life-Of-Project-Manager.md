There is a brief, magical window of time every morning right after the first black coffee hits my bloodstream. The sun is shining, the Wi-Fi is stable, and I look at my meticulously crafted daily agenda with the naive optimism of a man who hasn't yet opened Microsoft Teams.

I am a Senior Technical Project Manager. My job is essentially to herd highly educated cats through a maze of enterprise IT infrastructure while pretending everything is fine.

Here is exactly how that usually goes.

<span style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold;">The Morning: Optimism and Other Traps</span>

**8:45 AM: The Brief Delusion**
I start the day by reviewing my priorities. I feel organized. I feel optimistic. I have a color-coded matrix. This feeling will age terribly within the next forty-five minutes.

**9:30 AM: The Hydra Meeting**
First sync of the day. The goal was to align on two deliverables. We somehow leave with seven new action items, three existential questions about our architecture, and two additional spin-off meetings. I am essentially a meeting-generator NPC at this point.

**10:30 AM: The Art of the Polite Nudge**
I send a message to follow up on a critical task that was supposed to be done yesterday. I type "Just bringing this to the top of your inbox!" which is corporate speak for "Please do the thing we agreed you would do so I don't have to color a cell red in my status report."

**11:00 AM: The "Quick Question"**
A developer pings me: "Hey, quick question..."
Let me save you 18 years of IT experience: There is no such thing as a quick question. A "quick question" is a trap door disguised as a greeting. It usually ends with me redesigning a workflow automation logic branch.

<span style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold;">The Afternoon: Lunch is a Myth</span>

**12:00 PM: The Great Shuffle**
I reprioritize the morning's priorities because the priorities have changed. The color-coded matrix from 8:45 AM is now completely obsolete.

**1:00 PM: Lunch? Adorable.**
I stare at my Tupperware. I substitute water with another black coffee and pure adrenaline.

**2:00 PM: The "Just One Little Thing" Incident**
A stakeholder casually asks if we can add "just one little button" to the UI. I take a deep breath and gently explain that adding just one little thing to the scope is, in fact, expanding the scope. The button requires a backend API change, a security review, and a sacrifice to the Agile gods. It is not just a button.

**3:00 PM: The Meta-Work**
I update the project plan to reflect the things that happened while I was busy updating the project plan. I am caught in a project management inception loop.

<span style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold;">The Evening: Retreat and Regret</span>

**4:00 PM: The Consensus Protocol**
I send out a written recap of everything we discussed in the 9:30 AM meeting, so everyone can agree on what we already agreed we just discussed. Documentation is just defense.

**5:00 PM: The Dashboard Irony**
An executive messages me asking for a high-level status update. I take a long, slow sip of my lukewarm coffee and gently point them toward the automated JIRA board I spent three weeks architecting specifically so they wouldn't have to ask me for status updates. 

**6:00 PM: The Final Mistake**
I close the laptop feeling oddly accomplished. I survived. The project is moving forward. I make the fatal error of opening Outlook or Teams on my phone one last time on the walk to the MTR / Bus stop. Immediate, soul-crushing regret.

I put the phone in my pocket. Tomorrow, we do it all again. 🙃
