**Act 1: The Diagnosis We Used to Mock**

Back in our late twenties and early thirties, “Midlife Crisis” was our favorite Swiss Army knife of petty insults.
Did a friend or colleague suddenly stop ordering biryani at a restaurant and start talking about "gut health"? Midlife crisis. Did a buddy refuse a third peg of whiskey at a party because of an early morning run? We looked at him with profound existential concern, whispering that the poor man was undergoing an untimely hormonal collapse.
And if we heard about some guy in our extended circle having a shady extramarital affair, nobody simply said, “Well, that's a terrible choice. What he did is wrong.” Nope. We’d just shrug and say, “Textbook midlife crisis.”
Fast forward to today, and well… here I am.
Let me be very clear about one thing: the affair part stays off the table forever. That’s a firm “no” in early life, midlife, late life, and any potential afterlife—wherever I go after that.
The rest of the checklist, however? I am checking the boxes like an over-caffeinated auditor on the last day of March.


**Act 2: Gym, Quit, Regret, Repeat**

My grand midlife journey started where all sensible midlife journeys start: at the gym.
I signed up full of good intentions. Three months in, corporate deadlines conspired against me, my calendar exploded with meetings that could have been three-word emails, and I quit, citing “severe time constraints.” I told myself I just didn't have the time.
Then my body sent me a reality check.
Suddenly, getting off the living room sofa sounded like bubble wrap being popped inside a sleeping bag. My knees developed their own acoustic frequency. Bending down to tie my shoelaces felt like a diplomatic negotiation. Pains in joints that I didn't even know had scientific names became my permanent background track. The body made it brutally clear: You are no longer running on factory-installed youth, my friend. Routine maintenance is mandatory.
So, with my tail between my legs and Volini in my tote bag, I crawled back to the gym. Re-enrolled. Now I stand among guys half my age who are pumping iron while I treat stretching as a matter of national security.


**Act 3: From Watercolors to Whiskey Sours**

When most people hit their existential milestone, they lean into dignified, socially certified passions. They take up canvas painting. They buy an acoustic guitar that sits peacefully in the living room corner like an overpriced wooden ornament. They photograph birds at dawn.
What did I pick up?
Mixology.
I don't have the suave, flair-bartending swagger of a hipster in a rooftop lounge. I’m not throwing silver shakers behind my back or lighting orange peels on fire with a blowtorch. But hand me some ice, a jigger, a lime, and a decent bottle, and I will craft a dangerously mean drink.
Of all the noble pursuits on this planet, my subconscious decided that shaking ethanol with citrus was the ultimate calling. But then again, in chemistry, alcohol is technically a solution. If spending forty minutes figuring out the right balance of sweet and sour on a quiet weekday evening isn’t a midlife crisis, nothing is.


**Act 4: Ditching the Hat**

Then comes the physical vanity—specifically, the hair.
For decades, my relationship with caps, hats, and fedoras was less about fashion and more about a defensive border protocol against hereditary thinning. In every single vacation photograph, family function, or weekend stroll, the headgear was glued to my scalp.
Family photo? Cap on. Sunny beach vacation? Cap on. Quick run to the grocery store? Cap on. It was my simple, low-effort way of hiding genetics doing its thing on my hairline.
Recently, though, I took a hard look in the mirror and decided hats just weren't cutting it anymore. I bit the bullet, signed up for some basic scalp therapy (no, not a full-blown surgical transplant, just dignified clinical rescue missions), and did the unthinkable: I retired the caps.
Stepping into the camera frame without fabric on my head felt like doing a tightrope walk without a harness. But there I was, striking a pose, desperately trying to look good in the camera roll. Classic, undeniable crisis behavior.


**Act 5: Where Are My Car Keys?**

And don’t even get me started on the mental RAM.
My brain now routinely returns a 404: File Not Found. I will walk into a room with the intense purpose of a man on a mission, stop dead in the center of the rug, and blank out entirely. Why am I here? Was it my phone charger? A glass of water? A crucial document? Nobody knows. The system just reboots.
Most days now, my mood feels like a quick flip between being mildly annoyed at small things and feeling a bit low for no good reason. The only real antidote is the simple, noisy, happy time spent laughing around the table with family and close friends.


**Facing It Head-On**

So here we are. The midlife crisis isn't an urban legend, a punchline from a 90s sitcom, or an abstract label for other people. It is as tangible as the creak in my right knee and the ice rattling in my shaker.
The goal now isn't to frantically run away from it, nor is it to treat the gym as a temporary panic response until the crisis blows over. The goal is to survive it with a grin, keep the joints oiled, sip a well-balanced cocktail or a clean pour of whiskey, and make sure that this time, the healthy habits actually stick for the long haul.

Caps off to the next chapter of life.
